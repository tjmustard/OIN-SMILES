## Patterns & Lessons

- **Spec-document status fields are not authoritative on their own.** Multiple files in this repo's `spec/compiled/` had internal `Status: Ready for Implementation` fields that were stale or actively misleading (one was self-evidently superseded by its own successor's metadata; another pointed at a dependency that had been dead for months). Always corroborate against `src/` and `git log`, not just the doc's own claims.
- **A prior bulk "commit and push" (`5c42db7`) had silently truncated several compiled specs to 0 bytes instead of deleting them** when their content was archived under a new name — a botched `/hyper-audit`-style archiving flow. Worth checking `spec/compiled/` periodically for zero-byte files as a cheap health check.
- **Don't trust a sub-agent's single-run test failure without reproducing it directly** — the Explore agent's one observed failure in the winding-plumbing tests did not reproduce on a direct re-run, and treating it as authoritative would have caused a misclassification of active work as stalled.
- **The worklog itself can drift out of sync with the actual working tree** — `spec/worklog/NOTES.md` said Phase-1 winding-plumbing execution hadn't started, but `git status` showed it already had (uncommitted). Cross-checking narrative worklogs against `git status`/`git diff` directly is a cheap and valuable sanity check before trusting a "next steps" note.
