OIN-SMILES v0.4.4 → v0.4.20 — accuracy & speed retrospective

OIN-SMILES · release retrospective · v0.4.4 → v0.4.20

Seventeen releases, two questions, and three scoreboards that were lying.

Accuracy and speed across every point release since v0.4.4 — including the release where the headline number fell ten points on purpose, the release that built a fix worth eight points and then refused to ship it, the release that found the "enantiomer" fixtures behind that refusal were a coin, the release that discovered ferrocene had been built with its rings floating off the iron while every test stayed green, and the release that found a porphyrin's "correct" string had been a lottery ticket.

Is it more accurate? Yes — 89.34% self-consistent, 80.60% VERIFIED at v0.4.20, from 77.16% / 69.24% at v0.4.14 on the same 5000-molecule cohort. Real gains landed in v0.4.4–v0.4.6; then the default path changed no answer for five releases while the figure fell from 82.80% to 72.46% at v0.4.8 — the measurement was corrected, not the software regressed. v0.4.13 moved it up first. v0.4.17 (+5.56 / +5.36), v0.4.18 (+4.22 / +3.80), v0.4.19 (+0.84 / +0.72) and v0.4.20 (+1.56 / +1.48) account for the rest, each projected from an A/B over its affected population before a full sweep confirmed it — or, at v0.4.19, explained row by row why it came in higher. The VERIFIED number is the one to quote: a neutral ruler that never imports the encoder, and the first figure here a lever cannot raise by teaching the re-encode to agree with a wrong structure.
Is it faster? Yes, corpus-wide: > 30 s went 994 → 678 → 579 → 521 → 506 → 460 across six sweeps. 19.88% → 9.20% of the cohort over 30 s; max 760 → 606 s; Σ elapsed 38.7 → 25.17 h from v0.4.14 on, where the sweeps share one configuration. The first step nobody claimed. The largest single per-molecule win (81.89 s → 10.87 s) still ships behind a lever that is off, and Goal B (max < 30 s) is not delivered.

01

Explain it like I'm five

Think of the project as a machine that turns a 3D molecule into a short piece of text, and back again. It works if the text you get out the second time is exactly the text you put in.

For two releases the machine genuinely got better. Then someone checked the scoreboard and found it was cheating: when the machine was asked "did you rebuild the molecule right?", it had been grading its own homework — looking at what it meant to build instead of what it actually built. Once that was fixed, the score dropped from 83 out of 100 to 72 out of 100.

The machine didn't get worse. The scoreboard got honest.

Then five releases in a row changed nothing about how the machine works. That sounds bad, but it's the opposite of sloppiness. Each one built the thing it was supposed to build, measured it properly, and in most cases discovered the idea was wrong and shelved it. One built a fix worth eight points and then found it worked by throwing away information — quietly turning left-handed molecules into right-handed ones. It was switched off.

The sixth one finally moved the score up: 72 out of 100 to 76. It did that by taking the switched-off fix and adding the safety check that tells its good half from its harmful half — then proving on two separate samples that the harmful half was gone. It also cancelled the expensive re-measurement it was supposed to run, because re-running a machine that makes slightly different choices each time would have added noise to the very number it was trying to read, and a cheaper check gave an exact answer.

Then the project rebuilt all 5000 molecules from scratch and re-graded them — 77 out of 100 — and spent three releases finding that the next points could not be reached by choosing better among what the machine already builds.

The fourteenth release did something nobody had done: before choosing what to fix, it gave every one of the 5000 molecules exactly one reason for failing, using rulers that never ask the machine to grade itself. The biggest block turned out to be filed under the wrong name — 252 molecules everyone thought the machine could not build were the text being written two ways for the same shape. And the earlier "safety check" against left/right mix-ups had been guarding a problem the text was creating for itself. Fix the rule and the check has nothing to do. Score: 77 to 83, the biggest single jump — and a stricter second score, where a rebuilt molecule must pass an independent inspection: 69 to 75.

The fifteenth took on the biggest thing the census did leave with the machine: about 340 molecules where a ring of carbons that should sit on the metal was built floating just off it. A ruler showed every candidate was built 0.7 Å too far out — two lines of borrowed code aimed the ring at the edge of the bonding range, then stopped it stepping closer. Even ferrocene, the textbook example, was being built wrong, and no test had looked. Fixing the two lines, then teaching the machine to notice when a ring never arrived and try once more, took the score 83 to 87 on the loose count and 75 to 78 on the strict one. It also found one of the project's own guard-rail tests cannot see this whole class of defect: it grades the machine on its own bond list, and 36% of the rows it passes fail the honest check. Left open on purpose, evidence attached.

The sixteenth fixed the text again, in three small places: a carbon written so the reader would add a hydrogen that was not there, two rings that swapped their labels, and a rule that snapped a real ligand bond to make room for the metal. One of those fixes had sat switched off for thirteen releases, because the test that judged it had been run mostly on molecules the machine could not build in time; a ruler that reads the text back without building anything showed it fixed 56 of 84. Score: 87 to 88, and 78 to 79 on the strict count — slightly above the forecast, because the machine re-reads its own candidate molecules while it builds, so changing how it reads changed what it built for 44 molecules whose text had not changed at all.

The seventeenth went after the molecules whose text changes when you merely renumber the atoms in the file, mostly nickel and zinc porphyrins. Taking one apart step by step, it found the reading rules had no way to write a nitrogen carrying a negative charge. A porphyrin needs two, so the machine fell back on a guess, and the guess depended on which atom the file listed first: the "correct" text it had been producing was a lottery ticket that happened to win. Three bigger versions of the fix broke more than they repaired; the narrow one took the score 88 to 89, and 79 to 81 on the strict count, and six crystal structures of one zinc porphyrin, which used to get five different texts, now get one. It also made one molecule take ten minutes instead of eighty seconds, and that is written down next to the gain.

So: more accurate, yes — measured by rebuilding everything, and now graded twice. And much harder to fool.

Explain it like I'm twenty

Two separate questions, two separate answers.

Accuracy

The v0.4.8 drop needs explaining, and it is not a regression. The harness had been scoring a round trip with get_oin_string(gen_result.mol, coords) — the generator's own bond graph. That is circular: gen_result.mol is precisely the artifact that would have to be wrong for the test to fail. FIYHUT_comp_0 ships both cyclopentadienyl rings 0.85 Å off the iron — ten bonded carbons down to zero — and scored a byte-exact pass.

Re-deriving the verdict from the generated coordinates alone moved 613 molecules down and 30 up. 72.46% and 82.80% describe the same underlying performance; only one of them is true.

The v0.4.13 rise deserves the same scrutiny, because this project has already raised byte_exact once by deleting information. The donor fold moves 393 molecules in one direction with the comparison key untouched — and collapses enantiomers in 221 of them, which neither the metric nor the key can see. What makes +3.42 trustworthy is not the transition matrix. It is that the parity veto zeroes the collapse on two independent draws (19 → 0 cat-only, 33 → 0 mixed) while the achiral population stays unmoved in both, and that every collapsing molecule is accounted for as a separated pair rather than a decline. 171 survivors was also reached from the opposite direction: v0.4.11 bounded the safe set from above at "at most ~172" by counting damage.

v0.4.14 found the hole in that reasoning — and v0.4.17 found the coin under it The offline re-score that licensed both v0.4.13 and v0.4.14 could not express a loss: it holds the generated structure fixed, so for a molecule that already round-trips it can only print "still fine". Measured end-to-end, v0.4.13 is ~+2.42, not +3.42 and v0.4.14 is +1.42, not +1.56.

Then v0.4.17 re-read the three "oracle-confirmed enantiomer" fixtures the veto was built on and promoted against. Under the shipped encoder a renumbered x already landed on its mirror's string 8, 7 and 6 times in 12. One renumbering per fixture had been drawn — a coin, tossed three times. The "221 of 393 collapse enantiomers" and the "19 → 0" above were reading it. The fold was over-folding — swapping symmetry buckets rather than ligand automorphisms — the veto was patching the over-fold, and the fix needed neither.

v0.4.17 and v0.4.18 are the first releases measured the way that lesson demands. Both ran a real harness A/B over the complete affected population — 504 movers derived from coordinates, then all 1,146 η-bound molecules — with both arms generated, both numbers reported, and a full sweep owed and paid at each promotion. The sweeps landed on the predictions: 82.72 against 82.62, 86.10 against 86.04, 86.94 against 87.00, every residual a row at the 300 s budget.

v0.4.19 and v0.4.20 kept the method and found where it still leaks. Both promoted encoder levers whose effect on the string is exact and cheap to measure: a changed-string set over all 5,000, a canonicality audit of 5,000 × 11 transforms, a live harness A/B over the changed set, and an offline re-score for every row whose string did not move. v0.4.19's sweep then landed above its projection (87.78 against 87.56), and the row-by-row comparison found why: the generator re-encodes its candidate conformers to decide which one to keep, so a lever that changes perception changes what the generator builds, even for a molecule whose input string is byte-identical. 44 of 4,891 unchanged-string rows built a different structure, and five verified passes became false passes on the way — v0.4.14's lesson, arriving from the encoder side. v0.4.20 built the instrument that counts it and read it off its own sweep (37 rows, net +13 / +12, no new VERIFIED false pass); that sweep landed at 89.34 against 89.32 and 80.60 against 80.50.

What actually improved most

Neither number. What improved most is the project's ability to tell a real result from a fake one. The unit suite went 551 → 1097, and the instruments added in that window — the honest re-score, the corpus encoder-identity gate, the two-arm byte-identity gate, the coordination-integrity check, the mirror audit, and from v0.4.17 the four-instrument census and its neutral ruler — are what caught the 10.34-point inflation, a 59%-false-positive cohort, a dead gate arm, an enantiomer collapse, and then the fact that the "enantiomer collapse" had been measured on a coin. Five releases that moved no points each removed a way of being wrong — and v0.4.13 is the return on that, banking a fix that could only ship once an instrument existed that could see its damage.

v0.4.17 added the rule that makes a headline mean something: carry a second number the lever cannot teach. byte_exact is self-agreement, and a canonicality lever can raise it by making the second encode agree with whatever was built. The census's VERIFIED predicate judges the structure with a ruler that never imports the encoder; a lever that moves both together is doing what it claims. It also found that a gate re-frozen from a predicted mover list had carried 15 wrong rows for four releases — a mover list is a sample frame, not a verdict — hence /refreeze-goldens from a full run, every promotion.

v0.4.18 then used that full run to find what the gate itself is blind to. ARM 2's round-trip field is computed through the generator's own bond graph — the pre-v0.4.8 scored path — so whether a ring sits 2.1 Å or 2.9 Å from its metal never enters. 138 of 198 η rows built a different structure and the gated field moved on 0. A construction regression passes ARM 2, and did, for every release that shipped ferrocene detached. The general form: a gate that scores an artifact by the artifact's own account of itself cannot see the class of defect where the account is wrong — met in the harness at v0.4.8 and in the project's own regression gate at v0.4.18.

v0.4.19 showed that the ruler you judge a lever with decides whether it ships. OIN_H_FAITHFUL was built at v0.4.6 and held off for thirteen releases on a round-trip A/B that "moved nothing" — over a population that was mostly generator timeouts, where a repaired string still goes nowhere. The census's parse-back ruler reads the string back to a graph with no 3D at all, which for an encoder change is exact, and it repaired 56 of 84. A lever can be refuted by an instrument that could not have seen it work.

v0.4.20 is the case for bisecting one molecule before measuring a thousand. Three read-only hypotheses about the fragile class had been refuted before the lane began. Running one of its molecules through twelve stages of perception, with a numbering-invariant summary printed at each, showed that the ordering was not the first defect: the bond-order search had no N⁻, so a porphyrin dianion had no legal answer, and the "correct" shipped string was a guess that happened to land. Then the whole cohort refuted the three obvious ways of fixing it before the narrow one shipped. One molecule finds the mechanism; only the cohort tells you what the fix breaks.

How to read these numbers

Four traps, all of which this project has already fallen into once and documented.

Trap 1 — the bucket reports are not a time series They come from five different cohorts: 6719 (v0.4.2 capstone), 3917 (v0.4.4 regression, deliberately adversarial), 936 (v0.4.5 re-baseline), 5000 (v0.4.6 seed-42 sweep) and 5000 (the v0.4.14 baseline sweep, which the v0.4.17, v0.4.18, v0.4.19 and v0.4.20 sweeps re-use). Every accuracy figure here carries its N and its cohort. Do not draw a line between two of them. Two comparisons are genuine like-for-like: scored-vs-honest at v0.4.8 and again at v0.4.14, each classifying the same 5000 reports two ways — 10.34 and 9.72 points. The v0.4.6 and v0.4.14 sweeps share a cohort and a seed but not a code version or a thread configuration: closer than anything before them, still not a series.

✅ From v0.4.14 on the sweeps of record are a series: one cohort, one seed, 6 shards, a 300 s budget, BLAS threads = 1, and the only variable is the code under test. 77.16 → 82.72 → 86.10 → 86.94 → 87.78 → 89.34 self-consistent (69.24 → 74.60 → 77.66 → 78.40 → 79.12 → 80.60 VERIFIED) is the first line on this page that may legitimately be drawn — and each step was projected by an A/B on its affected population before the sweep confirmed it. ⚠ One exception in load, not configuration: the v0.4.19 sweep shared the machine with another job. The generator's timeout is advisory, so load can move accuracy too; that sweep's row-by-row comparison with its projection put 5 of its 40 differences on the 300 s budget boundary and the rest on generator reach.

✅ Every figure in that list is independently checkable. All five cohorts are frozen as per-molecule extracts and each re-derives its published headline exactly — 81.19 / 44.91 / 60.26 / 82.80 / 72.46 / 77.16, six for six; the v0.4.18 release headline re-derives from its frozen ruler verdicts, parse-back and honest bucket report alone (5000 / 4347 / 3920), and so do the v0.4.19 and v0.4.20 headlines (5000 / 4389 / 3956 and 5000 / 4467 / 4030, checked for this refresh). Every runtime row through v0.4.18 re-derives from the extracts; ⚠ the v0.4.19 and v0.4.20 rows do not — no per-molecule extract was frozen for either sweep. The sample definitions (seed, N, dedup priority, every molecule name) are frozen beside them. Untracked until v0.4.16.
Trap 2 — metrics.elapsed_s is nested, and it is a sum Read from the top level it silently yields 0. It also accumulates up to three separately SIGKILLed harness attempts, so the retired headline "max 759.9 s against a 300 s budget" was arithmetic on a sum — all 4658 single-attempt rows in the 5k sweep finish within 0.2 s of their cap. The harness enforces to ε ≈ 0.2 s.
Trap 3 — suite count is a rigour proxy, not accuracy 551 → 1097 tests means the project can detect more kinds of wrongness. It does not mean the notation got better. And a green suite is only as good as what it looks at: 1,067 of 1,067 tests passed while ferrocene was being built with its rings 0.8 Å off the iron, because no test measured that distance.
Trap 4 — from v0.4.17 there are two numbers, and byte_exact is the weaker one Self-consistent (byte_exact under the honest score) is self-agreement: E(x) == E(G(E(x))). VERIFIED additionally requires that the string parses back to the input's graph and that a neutral ruler — one that never imports the encoder — finds the generated structure graph-isomorphic to the input with no stereo element mirrored or changed. The gap between them (406 passes at v0.4.17, 427 at v0.4.18, 433 at v0.4.19, 437 at v0.4.20) is accuracy the metric asserts, not accuracy the project has. A lever must move both, or it may be teaching the second encode to agree with a wrong structure. Every figure on this page before v0.4.17 is self-consistent only; the 77.16% baseline is 69.24% VERIFIED.

Each card: what shipped · accuracy · speed · what was refuted. Refutations get equal weight to wins — that is what most of these releases produced.

v0.4.4

Accuracy, and the instrument to measure it

Accuracy gain Speed gain

Six parallel worktree swimlanes. The centrepiece is a fac/mer-aware canonical round-trip key that separates a genuine fac↔mer miss from benign slot relabeling, plus the bucket report that decomposes every round trip into six classes.

Accuracy
Real gain. OIN_EARLY_EXIT — accept the first conformer that independently re-encodes to the key, instead of exhausting the pool — took byte-exact 44.7% → 60.5% and key-match 55.3% → 73.7% with zero regressions. A separate 3917-molecule regression sweep measured 11 regressions / 1092 fixes, and all 11 regressions are 300 s timeouts: zero correctness regressions.
Speed
Real gain. OIN_EARLY_EXIT is ~5× faster on the worst cohort. Encoder-side, the AC2BO valence-combination sort was capped and resonance enumeration moved into a forked, CPU-bounded child, recovering previously-unencodable molecules without changing any encodable OIN.
Refuted
Rigid η-winding construction, Kabsch greedy placement, and a stretched-bond acceptance metric all measured net-negative or neutral and kept OFF — a third independent confirmation that selection beats construction in this pipeline.
Caution
The byte_exact 81.19% figure often associated with v0.4.4 is the v0.4.4 key applied to v0.4.2-generated data. It measures the new classifier, not new generation.

v0.4.5

The emitted string becomes canonical

Accuracy gain Speed gain

Six canonicality levers promoted to default-ON together, via a new single-source registry. What made them safe as a block: each repairs a renumbered presentation without rewriting the canonical answer — which is why the corpus shows no churn.

Accuracy
Real gain — and the important number is not byte_exact. On a generator-free probe holding the graph fixed and varying only rotation and atom renumbering (so the correct answer is byte-identical by construction): byte-stability 58.1% → 69.6%, comparison-key instability 60 → 16 molecules. The key is the harness's acceptance predicate and the basis of every accuracy number the project reports — it went from unstable on 1 molecule in 5 to 1 in 19. On the 936-molecule re-baseline, 145 of 436 previously-failing molecules were fixed.
Speed
Real gain, unconditional. The confirming step of OIN_EARLY_EXIT — a full re-perception measured at 48–57 s per call — was running twice per conformer, once during pool fill and again, redundantly, in the selection re-scan. Refresh the report at any release close-out. The command re-derives the figures from the sources, appends one release section, updates the verdicts and the gap table, and republishes this page to the same URL. 

/release-retrospective v0.4.21      # append one release (the common case)

It lives at .claude/commands/release-retrospective.md, and it carries the standing caveats with it — cohorts are not comparable, elapsed_s is nested and a sum, the corpus speed figure is stale until a sweep says otherwise, a lever measured but shipped default-OFF changes nothing for a user, and from v0.4.17 every accuracy figure is two numbers.
